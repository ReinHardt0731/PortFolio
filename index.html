AIRCRAFT DESIGN / ANALYSIS 1,517.981 lbAircraft gross weight 14.89 lbf/ft2Maximum allowable wing loading 8.28 lbf/hpMaximum power loading under its sizing constraint REPORTED SIZING LIMITS Study outline

GANNET-T731 is a trainer-aircraft concept developed from client-defined performance requirements. The study connects mission drivers to constraint-based sizing, then compares propeller performance, airfoil choices, and wing geometry. The mission profile frames the sizing limits, and the figures below show the supporting calculations and plots.

MISSION PROFILE

Performance requirements across the flight

01 / REQUIREMENT-BASED SIZING

Identify feasible wing and power loading.

Stall speed, maximum speed, take-off, rate of climb, and service-ceiling requirements define constraints on power loading versus wing loading. Their intersections set sizing limits. The report gives a maximum allowable wing loading of 14.89 lbf/ft² and a maximum power loading of 8.28 lbf/hp under their respective constraints.

02 / AIRFOIL SELECTION

Balance aerodynamic performance and structural needs.

I set custom weights for maximum lift, minimum drag, lift-to-drag ratio, thickness, pitching moment, and angle-of-attack margin for each wing section. This let me compare candidate airfoils by both aerodynamic performance and the greater thickness required near the wing root.

03 / PROPELLER & EXCESS POWER

Compare available and required power across airspeed.

Excess power is the difference between power available and power required. This sea-level graph compares those curves across airspeed; positive excess power indicates power available for climb and acceleration. Dashed limits mark the operating speeds and maximum power.

04 / WING OPTIMIZATION

Measure how wing geometry affects aerodynamic response.

Response Surface Methodology compared taper ratio, wing twist, and section span distribution across aerodynamic responses. The report’s R² analysis identifies twist and section span distribution as the strongest influences; taper ratio has a smaller and less consistent effect in this study.
